# digital-museum
Museum of Faded Things is an interactive digital museum of everyday objects that once filled Indian homes, offices and streets between 1950 and 2010, and have since faded from daily life.

Nine objects, each in 3D, form the collection: the rotary telephone, valve radio, typewriter, film camera, inland letter, black-and-white TV, cassette tape, Walkman and STD/PCO booth. You can turn each one with your finger, tap any part to see it glow and learn what it did, and trigger its signature action with sound, such as dialling the phone, tuning the radio or rewinding the cassette. Each object has a short story about why it faded, plus a "Did you know?" fact checked against sources where possible.

The page opens on an intro screen and moves through the collection like a gallery. Arrows and dots take you from object to object, a pull-up sheet holds the story, and a 1950 to 2010 slider shows which objects were in everyday use in any year. The look is warm and vintage, set in Cinzel and EB Garamond on a dark gallery stage.

Why it exists: most nostalgia is just a list of photos. This turns it into something you can handle, hear and explore, so that people who remember these objects feel it, and people who don't can see what they were.

What it's built with: a single web page using Three.js for the 3D objects, with sound generated in the browser. It needs no downloads, models or backend.
